How to Get Your Music on Spotify Playlists (Without Getting Scammed)

Playlists are still the fastest way to put your music in front of new listeners on Spotify. But the space is full of bad advice and outright scams. Here's how playlists actually work in 2026 — and how to grow the right way.

The three types of Spotify playlists

Not all playlists are equal. There are three kinds, and they're reached in completely different ways:

  • Editorial playlists — curated by Spotify's own team (think RapCaviar, lofi beats). You reach these only by pitching through Spotify for Artists before your release date.
  • Algorithmic playlists — generated for each listener (Discover Weekly, Release Radar, Radio). These are driven by real listener behaviour — saves, repeat listens, and shares.
  • User-curated playlists — built by independent curators and networks (like Higher Vision). These are where most independent artists actually get their first traction.

How to actually get placed

There's no secret button — there's a process:

  • Pitch editorial early. Submit unreleased tracks via Spotify for Artists at least 7 days before release. Even if you don't get an editorial spot, pitching tells the algorithm what your song is.
  • Earn the algorithm. Algorithmic playlists react to genuine engagement. Drive your existing audience to save and repeat the track in week one — that signal compounds.
  • Submit to real curators. Find independent curators in your exact niche and send a short, personal message with the track. Quality of fit beats quantity of submissions.
  • Be consistent. One song rarely breaks an artist. A steady release cadence gives the algorithm more chances to find your audience.

The scams to avoid

If a service guarantees playlist placement, streams, or followers in exchange for money — walk away. That's "pay-for-placement" (payola), and it violates Spotify's rules. The cost isn't just wasted money:

  • Spotify removed tens of millions of tracks tied to artificial streaming and now applies penalties to flagged content.
  • Bot streams earn no royalties, don't count toward charts, and can get your music removed.
  • Fake-follower playlists have followers but no real listeners — the "follower-to-listener" mismatch is exactly what gets a playlist delisted.

The honest model: pay a curator for their time and an honest review if you want feedback — never for a guaranteed slot. Real growth comes from real listeners.

What actually moves the needle

Make something genuinely good, target the right niche playlists (a focused 5,000-follower playlist where people actually listen beats a botted 50,000-follower one), drive real first-week engagement, and stay consistent. That's it. There's no shortcut that doesn't eventually cost you.
